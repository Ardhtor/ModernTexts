# Kirk: Perception in Real Time

## Scenario model

This is an experiential model rather than a conventional third-person story. The viewer occupies the perceptual position of Joseph while Kirk's physical growth progressively changes the room, the objects within it, and the viewer's interpretation of both.

You enter the room.

Kirk is already there.

Nothing is remarkable yet. The room establishes its ordinary scale: doorway, ceiling, chair, table, floor, distance between bodies. These things quietly define what "normal" means.

Then you notice Kirk.

He is larger than the room seems prepared for.

You look from him to the doorway.

Back to him.

The doorway has not changed.

Kirk has.

That distinction becomes the first fact you cannot stop noticing.

You begin using the room to measure him without consciously deciding to. His shoulders occupy more of the doorway. His position changes the apparent dimensions of the room. Furniture that seemed ordinary a moment ago acquires a different scale when he stands beside it.

You look at the chair.

You look at Kirk.

The chair is suddenly evidence.

You remember Kirk fitting into it differently.

Then the perception becomes recursive: you are no longer merely seeing Kirk. You are seeing the difference between Kirk now and Kirk as encoded in the objects around him.

The world has become a measuring instrument.

Kirk grows again.

There is no dramatic announcement. The change is first registered through the environment. A piece of clothing sits differently. The available space around him contracts. His movement has a different relationship to the furniture. The ceiling feels lower.

You notice before you understand.

Then you understand, and that understanding changes what you notice next.

You begin scanning automatically.

Kirk.

Door.

Chair.

Floor.

Kirk.

Your own body enters the comparison.

You are now another measurement.

Kirk grows again.

The room accommodates him because it has to. Furniture moves. Objects become inadequate. Some things are replaced. Some remain behind.

The discarded objects acquire a strange temporal quality. They are physical records of earlier versions of Kirk.

You can reconstruct his growth from the room.

That means the room remembers.

Kirk is no longer merely larger. His previous sizes remain materially present as traces.

You begin anticipating the next change.

You look at the ceiling before Kirk reaches it.

You look at the doorway before he approaches it.

You notice which objects will become insufficient next.

Your perception has adapted to Kirk's growth.

Your cognition has begun running ahead of your senses.

Kirk grows again.

Your predictions become obsolete.

You update them.

He grows again.

You update them again.

Eventually the experience stops feeling like a sequence of isolated transformations. It becomes a moving baseline.

Every new size becomes ordinary for a moment.

Then the ordinary becomes inadequate.

Then the world changes.

Then the changed world becomes ordinary.

Then Kirk changes again.

The loop is:

`Kirk grows → the environment becomes relatively smaller → you perceive the discrepancy → your mental model expands → the environment is adapted → the adapted environment becomes the new baseline → Kirk grows again.`

The important asymmetry remains intact: Kirk is the physical growth process; you are the perceptual and cognitive process responding to it.

But the environment is not passive. It is the third participant. It supplies constraints, measurements, memories, and consequences. It is altered by Kirk and then becomes evidence through which you perceive Kirk's next transformation.

Eventually you realize that you have stopped asking how large Kirk is.

You are asking what the world will have to become to contain him.

Then you realize that this question is itself part of the process.

You look at the room differently because you know Kirk will grow again.

Kirk grows.

The room changes.

You change your model of the room.

The changed room changes your model of Kirk.

The changed model changes what you notice.

What you notice changes what the room means.

And the process continues.

There is no final frame.

There is only another baseline.

`B₀ → growth → disruption → perception → adaptation → B₁ → growth → disruption → perception → adaptation → B₂ → …`

The viewer never escapes the loop. The world itself becomes the interface through which Kirk's growth is experienced.
